Working the Kenetik booth

For anyone working a Kenetik booth: the two-sentence pitch, the questions people ask and what to say, and the do's and don'ts. Read it once before your shift. It takes five minutes.
The checklist
How we think about Kenetik
Brain fuel, not an energy drink. A calm, steady lift for the part of the day that still needs you, with nothing in it to keep you up tonight.

Say it rightKenetik is said “kuh-NET-ik,” like kinetic. Ketones is said “KEE-tones.”

The 3 angles

How to close

Point them to drinkkenetik.com before they walk away, and tell them about any offer we're running at the event.

Questions you'll get
So what is it?
Brain fuel. A drink made with 12 grams of ketones and no caffeine, for clear, steady focus.
Is it an energy drink?
No. There's no caffeine in our cans. Ketones are a different kind of fuel, so it's a calm, steady lift rather than a jolt.
What are ketones?
A fuel your body makes on its own, for example when you fast. Every can has 12 grams, and the shots have 10.
Does it have caffeine?
The cans have none. For a shot, read the label on the one in your hand before you answer.
How much sugar is in it?
Point them to the nutrition panel on the can rather than answering from memory.
When do I drink it?
Whenever you want a clear head: the afternoon slump, before deep work, or in place of a second coffee.
Which flavor should I try?
Ask what they usually drink and start them on the flavor closest to it. Then let them try a second.
Will it help with a health issue I have?
We can't speak to any health condition, and we don't give health advice. Kenetik is brain fuel for everyday focus, and their doctor is the right person to ask.
Can my kids have it, or can I have it while pregnant?
We don't give health advice. The label has the details, and their doctor is the one to ask.
Where can I buy it?
At drinkkenetik.com, on Amazon and on TikTok Shop.
Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Hand them a sample first, then talk. The taste does most of the work.
  • Say brain fuel and keep the pitch to two sentences.
  • Ask what they usually drink in the afternoon and start there.
  • Send every question about health, sugar or ingredients to the label or their doctor.
  • Send them to drinkkenetik.com before they walk away.

Don't

  • Call it an energy drink or a supplement.
  • Say it treats, cures or helps any health condition, or give health advice.
  • Answer sugar or caffeine questions about a shot from memory. Point to its label.
  • Promise anything about kids or pregnancy. That's their doctor's call.
  • Mix it into a cocktail, or talk down another brand.
