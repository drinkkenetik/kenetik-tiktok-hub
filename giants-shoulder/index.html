Ad Read Talking Points

No script. Three reads, one angle each, told the way you would explain it on the show. The examples below only show the idea.
How we think about Kenetik
Caffeine-free brain fuel for afternoon clarity and focus, without messing with your sleep.
The goal
We want your listeners to come away knowing their brain has a second fuel, and to reach for Kenetik the next time the afternoon pull toward a coffee hits.
The 3 angles
1
Your brain has a second fuel
WhyYour audience comes for how the brain actually works, so lead with the mechanism, not a pitch. The brain runs mostly on glucose, but it also runs on ketones, the fuel the body makes when you fast. Most people have never given theirs the second one. Kenetik is 12 grams of ketones in a can, with no caffeine. Explain it the way you would on the show, then say how you drink it.
What it could sound like (not a script)“Most people think the brain runs on sugar, full stop. But it has a second fuel. When you fast, your body makes ketones, and your brain runs on them too. Kenetik is 12 grams of ketones in a can, so I don't have to skip lunch to give my brain the other one.”
2
Coffee hides the tiredness
WhyYour listeners will enjoy this one. The caffeine in coffee doesn't give the brain anything to run on. It blocks adenosine, the molecule that builds up through the day and tells you that you're tired, and it can still be doing that at bedtime. Say it as a fact, not a knock on coffee: most of your audience drinks it, and so might you. Then the turn: in the afternoon, give your brain actual fuel instead.
What it could sound like (not a script)“Fun fact: coffee doesn't actually give your brain any fuel. It just blocks the signal that you're tired. So in the afternoon I give my brain real fuel instead. Kenetik is caffeine-free, so there's nothing in it keeping me up that night.”
3
What you noticed
WhyYou asked to drink it for a few weeks before you read for it, and that is the read your audience will trust most. Say when you actually drink it, prepping an episode, reading papers, a late edit, and what your afternoons feel like now. Your own experience only, with no promises about what it will do for anyone else.
What it could sound like (not a script)“I drank this for a few weeks before I agreed to talk about it. It's become my three o'clock thing when I'm prepping an episode. My head's clear, I feel calm, and there's no four o'clock coffee I end up regretting at midnight.”

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Place each read in a neuroscience or cognition episode, not one about psychedelics.
  • Lead with how it works, then how you use it. Your audience trusts the mechanism more than a pitch.
  • Say caffeine-free and 12 grams of ketones. Those two facts carry every read.
  • Keep each read to one angle, 60 to 90 seconds.
  • If you want to cite a study, send it to us first so we can check it together.

Don't

  • Call it an energy drink. It's caffeine-free brain fuel.
  • Make any health or medical claim, or say it helps you sleep.
  • Read it off a page. Tell it from how you actually use it.

The bottom line

Your brain has a second fuel, and Kenetik is the caffeine-free way to give it some. If listeners remember one thing, that is it. How you explain it is up to you.
