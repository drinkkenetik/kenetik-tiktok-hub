Every version

Do

  • Make monke a woman in her late forties who is visibly good at what she does. Open on her mid-sentence in front of people.
  • Play the lost word straight and hold it. The joke is never on monke.
  • Clear the fog on the sip, and make the can’s crack the loudest sound in the video.
  • Keep the science plain: a battery, a fuel line, a second fuel line. No cartoon hormones and no anatomy.
  • Burn the on-screen text in, big and readable. Most people watch with the sound off.
  • Deliver 9:16 first, with a 60-second cutdown.

Don't

  • Call it an energy drink or a supplement. It’s brain fuel.
  • Show or say that Kenetik treats, fixes or prevents menopause, brain fog or memory loss, or that it helps you sleep.
  • Say or show sugar-free or zero sugar.
  • Put a hormone patch, a prescription or a doctor in the noise scene. The noise is products, never medical care.
  • Add a before-and-after or a transformation montage. She finishes her sentence and carries on.
  • Open on monke in a kitchen or alone. The first frame shows her doing something she’s good at.
  • Say “your menopause” to the viewer, or put an age on her. Monke’s age is monke’s.

The bottom line

She didn’t lose it. Her brain was short on fuel it can use. Name the stage, cut through the noise, explain the fuel, then the can. No promises: it’s not a cure, it’s fuel.
