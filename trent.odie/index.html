Your brief, Trent

Hotshot trucking, truck camping and cooking on the road, raw and real.
The moment

A long haul ahead, or a late night editing after one. You already drink coffee, so Kenetik is how you lock back in without stacking more caffeine on top.

Who you're talking to

People who ride along with your days: drivers, campers, and anyone running a business from the road who knows what hour nine feels like.

Your angle

Keep it the way your channel already works. Kenetik shows up inside the day, never as a stop for an ad. The story is staying dialed in through the drive, the filming and the edit without the jitters or the afternoon crash.

What a great video covers
1Hook

Open in the first 1 to 3 seconds with one of your lines below.

2The problem

Hour nine of the drive, a load to deliver, footage to edit tonight, and a brain going in a million directions.

3Kenetik

Crack one open, show your honest take. Say "brain fuel" once. Caffeine-free is the twist.

4Close

One reason to try it (caffeine-free, 12g of ketones) and your code ODIE or your link.

Day-in-the-life with talk to camera and voiceover is your format. Work Kenetik into a moment that already happens: the fuel stop, the cab before a long stretch, the laptop after dark.

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Say brain fuel once.
  • Keep it inside the day, the way you already film.
  • Mention coffee as what you already drink, not as the enemy.
  • Show yourself drinking it in the cab.

Don't

  • Call it an energy drink (you're contrasting against them).
  • Make any health or medical claim.
  • Open with caffeine or ketones.
  • Over-produce it.
  • Cram everything into the opener.

The bottom line

Your lane is the owner-operator who stays dialed in from the first mile to the last edit, on clean brain fuel. Keep it raw, keep it inside the day, and let the drive make the point.
