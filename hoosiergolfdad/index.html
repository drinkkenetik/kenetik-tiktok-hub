Your brief, Aaron

Golf challenges, built around the holes where focus decides the score.
The moment

The back nine. The swing still feels fine, but the reads get lazy and the decisions start slipping. That is your window: you reach for Kenetik at 7am and again mid afternoon, and it keeps you clear through the last six holes.

Who you're talking to

Weekend golfers and golf dads who love a challenge format and want to play the last six holes as sharp as the first six. Most of them run on coffee and feel the fade every round.

Your angle

Your story is the swap. You were leaning hard on caffeine, you cut it completely, and your motivation and focus went up. Lead with the challenge, let the round prove the focus, and drop the swap story in once people are already watching.

What a great video covers
1Hook

Open in the first 1 to 3 seconds with one of your lines below.

2The problem

The late-round fade: a lazy read on the green, a rushed swing on 16, the double you didn't need.

3Kenetik

Crack one open, show your honest take. Say "brain fuel" once. Caffeine-free is the twist.

4Close

One reason to try it (caffeine-free, 12g of ketones) and your code GOLFDAD or your link.

Challenge rounds are your strongest format. Keep the scorecard on screen, keep a can in frame at the turn, and save any talking-head review for the cooldown at the end.

Your 5 hooks
1
“Driver only for eighteen holes. The hard part isn't the swing, it's staying locked in on the back nine.”
Video ideaDriver-only challenge round with a running scorecard. Crack a Kenetik at the turn and keep the cut to the back nine.
Why it worksA rule the viewer can check keeps them watching to the last hole, and the back nine is where everyone has felt the fade.
2
“Range ball versus Pro V1, nine holes each. Same golfer, same focus all day.”
Video ideaSplit-screen scorecard, range ball front nine, Pro V1 back nine. Kenetik out at the turn between the nines.
Why it worksThe comparison is the hook, so the product can sit in the background. Viewers stay to see the result, and the can is in every shot of the second half.
3
“I quit caffeine this year and my golf got better. Here's the round that proved it.”
Video ideaTalk to camera at the first tee for one line, then cut to the round. End on the scorecard.
Why it worksA personal change plus a measurable result is a story people finish. Keep it to your own round, never a promise about theirs.
4
“Hole fourteen is where my round usually falls apart. Not today.”
Video ideaOpen on the tee box at 14 with the scorecard showing, then play it out. Show the can earlier in the round.
Why it worksNaming the exact hole makes every golfer check their own scorecard in their head, and the tension carries the clip.
5
“The 7am can and the 2pm can. That's my whole golf routine now.”
Video ideaDay-in-the-life: morning can before the range, afternoon can before the back nine, quick cuts of the shots in between.
Why it worksA routine is easy to copy, and the times make it concrete. It shows when you drink it without having to explain anything.
Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Say brain fuel once.
  • Keep the scorecard on screen for challenge rounds.
  • Tell the caffeine swap as your own story.
  • Show yourself drinking it at the turn.

Don't

  • Call it an energy drink (you're contrasting against them).
  • Make any health or medical claim.
  • Open with caffeine or ketones.
  • Over-produce it.
  • Cram everything into the opener.

The bottom line

Your lane is the golfer who plays the back nine as sharp as the front, on clean brain fuel. Lead with the challenge, prove the focus on the scorecard, and tell the caffeine swap once.
