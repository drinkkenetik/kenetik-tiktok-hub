Your 5 hooks
01
“Energy drinks make me sleepier. This is the only thing that keeps me sharp on a ride.”
Video idea: Voiceover over riding b-roll, crack a Kenetik before you roll out.
The line contradicts something everyone assumes is true, and a contradiction makes a viewer stop to test it against his own experience. Keep it firmly yours — you got sleepier, so you swapped — because that is a story rather than an argument. The road footage gives it stakes nothing else on your feed has.
02
“Before a ride, this is what I reach for. Clear head, more alert, no crash.”
Video idea: Garage-to-road b-roll with a talk-over, can in the shot.
Riding is one of the few settings where a clear head is obviously not optional, so the stakes are already in frame and you never have to argue for them. Say the line once and let the ride carry it. Do not add a safety claim; it is not ours to make.
03
“Wrenching all day in the garage. This keeps my head clear when I need to focus.”
Video idea: Show the build, voiceover the focus angle, Kenetik on the bench.
Build footage earns a watch on its own, so the drink gets carried along by something the viewer already wanted to see. Showing it on the bench proves the habit lives outside the ride, which is what separates a routine from a one-off. Keep the word focus in it.
04
“I stopped drinking energy drinks. Turns out clean brain fuel actually keeps me awake.”
Video idea: Honest talk-to-camera moment, then back to the bike.
“Turns out” does quiet work here — it signals you were not expecting this, so the viewer hears a discovery instead of an endorsement. Surprise is the most credible position a paid creator can occupy. Say the doubt you had first, and do not smooth it out in the edit.
05
“Clear thinking on two wheels matters. This is the caffeine-free fuel I run on.”
Video idea: Ride b-roll, one clean line as the hook, sip through the day.
“Run on” is your world's vocabulary applied to yourself, and borrowed language like that lands because it does not sound like copy. Caffeine-free is the part that makes a rider pause, since he assumed alert meant caffeinated. One clean line over the ride is enough; it does not need explaining.
Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Say "brain fuel" once.
  • Open on the energy-drink contrast, it is your best angle.
  • Use your b-roll and voiceovers, your strength.
  • Show the garage and the ride.
  • Show yourself actually drinking it.

Don't

  • Call it an energy drink (you're contrasting against them).
  • Make any health or medical claim.
  • Open with caffeine or ketones.
  • Over-produce it.
  • Cram everything into the opener.

The bottom line

Your lane is the rider who found energy drinks made him sleepy and swapped in clean brain fuel for a clear head. Hook on the contrast, show the ride or the build, say brain fuel once. The videos that land, we run as paid ads, and that is how you keep earning.
