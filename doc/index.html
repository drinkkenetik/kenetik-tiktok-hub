The goal

We are testing hooks for your streams and clips. The first few seconds decide whether a viewer stays, so drop these at the top of a clip or work them into a stream moment. We will watch what lands and lean into the winners.

What Kenetik is

Kenetik is metabolic brain fuel: clean, caffeine-free focus. It is the edge that keeps your mind sharp when it matters. Lead with the win or the moment, then bring in Kenetik as the fuel behind it.

Your audience

Your viewers do not all come for the same thing. Some want to game at a higher level, some want to clean up their wellness, and some want to get more done in their day. We built a tab for each, all in your voice, so you can speak to whoever is watching.

One rule

Pick the tab that fits your audience, open with the problem, show Kenetik as the fix, and say brain fuel once. Keep it real. The videos that land, we run as paid ads, and that is how everyone earns more.

The hooks
01
“The clearest mind wins the gunfight. Simple as that. I fuel mine with Kenetik.”
Flex / clarity is the edge · Clip intro · Competitive gamers
Your audience does not come to you for evidence, they come for verdicts, so a flat declarative line does more work than any explanation would. Focus is our second-best theme at 13 percent conversion. Deliver it as a rule you already live by and let Kenetik be the fuel behind it.
02
“The Two-Time does not get out-aimed. A clear head is the edge, and this is what I run on. Caffeine-free brain fuel.”
Flex / dominance · Stream intro · Competitive gamers
The boast is the mechanism. When the guy who never loses names what he runs on, the viewer is buying the standard rather than the ingredient list, so do not soften it into a recommendation. Land “caffeine-free brain fuel” as the last three words, and keep it framed as what you run on.
03
“You want faster reactions? It starts in your head. Fuel your brain, win more gunfights.”
Performance / how-to · To-cam · Competitive gamers
A question puts the viewer into an answer-seeking posture, which is the only reason a how-to earns a watch off a cold scroll. The reframe is what makes it yours — everyone else sells hands, you are selling the head behind them. Keep it to how you prepare, and never promise anyone faster reactions.
04
“Every lobby is mine to dominate. The difference is focus, and focus is fuel. Say hello to Kenetik.”
Flex / category · Mid-stream · Gamers
Three beats, each shorter than the last, and the product arrives at the peak instead of the open. That ordering matters: the viewer has already agreed with you twice before Kenetik enters the frame. Film it mid-stream with the game audio live, because a clean studio take of this line reads as an ad.
05
“Champions do not crash at hour six. I stay sharp start to finish. No caffeine, no crash.”
Endurance / stream stamina · Mid-stream · Streamers
No-crash is our weakest theme by a distance, 7 percent conversion and about $18 a video, so the endurance flex has to carry the open and the crash line can only ride out at the tail. Hour six is the detail that sells it. Say the number, and speak about your own streams.
06
“Everyone is chasing the next energy drink. I fuel the one thing that actually wins games. My brain.”
Category stake / contrast · To-cam · Gamers
Naming the category you are not in is a status move, and it separates you from every sponsored can your audience has already tuned out. Caffeine-swap content converts at 12 percent for us. The line only holds if Kenetik stays on the other side of that sentence — brain fuel, never an energy drink.
07
“Speed. Violence. Momentum. None of it matters if your mind is a step behind. This keeps mine razor sharp.”
Persona / brain fuel · To-cam · Gamers
Your own catchphrase is the pattern-interrupt. The audience mouths along for three words, then you break the rhythm with a fourth thing they were not expecting, and that turn is the entire video. Hit the three beats hard, drop your volume on the line about the mind, and let the contrast do the work.
08
“New challenger in the lobby? Good. A fueled brain is a faster brain, and mine is fueled. Let’s go.”
Flex / edge · Mid-stream · Gamers
This is not a read, it is a moment, and that is exactly why it converts. Raw one-take footage outperformed polished ad-style content for every brand we audited, and a line delivered while a lobby is actually loading cannot be rebuilt in post. Capture it live or leave this one alone.

Do

  • Say "brain fuel" once.

Don't

03
“Taking care of your mind is a flex. Clean fuel, caffeine-free, every day. That is how I stay dialed.”
Identity / ritual · To-cam · Wellness-minded viewers
Redefining a behavior as status is how you get a competitive audience to adopt it. Nobody in your comments is looking for a routine, but every one of them wants a flex. The daily part is what turns a one-off video into something a viewer can picture having, so say when you actually drink it.
04
“Sugar-free, caffeine-free, nothing artificial. Even the Two-Time reads the label. This is clean fuel.”
Category / proof · First-sip · Wellness-minded viewers
Bloom's biggest videos are a stack of hard facts delivered fast, and that rhythm works here because three verifiable things land quicker than one argument. The label line is the joke that keeps it from sounding like an ad read. Hold the can up while you say it and keep it one take.
05
“Wellness is not soft. A clear, calm mind is how you win the long game. This is what I drink.”
Reframe / flex · To-cam · Wellness-minded viewers
This one answers the objection your audience actually has, which is that wellness is for other people. Naming it out loud and refusing it is more persuasive than any benefit you could list underneath. Deliver it straight — wink at the camera here and the whole thing reads as a bit, and nothing carries.
06
“I swapped the energy drinks for brain fuel and my head has never been clearer. No crash.”
Switch / testimonial · To-cam · Caffeine-cutters
A switch story is credible because it costs you something: you are admitting what you used to run on. Keep the entire line in first person, reporting your own experience rather than telling anyone what will happen to them. The crash mention belongs at the end of the sentence and never at the open.
07
“Your brain is the most important muscle you own. Fuel it clean. This is how I take care of mine.”
Category stake · To-cam · Wellness-minded viewers
Arguing for the category before the brand is a leader's move, and we can afford it — Kenetik leads ketones on TikTok Shop by roughly 14x. A performance-minded audience already accepts that muscle needs fuel. Say how you take care of yours; do not describe what it does to a brain.
08
“Feeling foggy and wired at the same time? That is your fuel. Clean it up with Kenetik.”
Callout / problem · To-cam · Wellness-minded viewers
Naming a state someone is in while they are scrolling is BrainMD's whole engine — the viewer answers yes before deciding whether to keep watching. Category / contrast · To-cam · Productivity-minded viewers
Correcting a belief is stickier than adding one, because the viewer has to check themselves against it. The not-this-but-that structure is easy to repeat, which is how a line travels from a video into the comments. “Simple” is the authority stamp at the end; do not soften it into a question.
05
“Hit the afternoon wall? Champions push through with clean focus, not another coffee.”
Callout / problem · To-cam · Working viewers
The afternoon wall does your audience targeting for you — anyone who has felt that hour recognizes themselves before a product is mentioned. 06
“Win your workday. Clear head, steady focus, no crash. That is what brain fuel does.”
Reframe / benefit · To-cam · Productivity-minded viewers

Reframe / crossover · To-cam · Productivity-minded viewers
A boss fight implies preparation, and that implication is doing the selling — your audience already accepts that you load out before a hard one. The three imperatives then give the video somewhere to go. Keep it under eight seconds, because the metaphor gets thin the moment you explain it.

Identity / flex · To-cam · Productivity-minded viewers
Lines built like proverbs get saved and re-sent in a way that product copy never does, and this one borrows a structure people already half-know, so it sounds familiar the first time they hear it. The last three words are what keep it from being a poster. Say them faster than the rest.
