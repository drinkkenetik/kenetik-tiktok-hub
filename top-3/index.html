What Kenetik is (and isn't)

What Kenetik is

Metabolic brain fuel: clean, caffeine-free focus in a can or a shot. Steady, calm clarity that lasts, without the crash. 12g of ketones, caffeine-free, sugar-free, nothing artificial. Always lead with the person's problem or the moment they feel it, then bring in Kenetik as the fix.

What Kenetik is NOT

Not an energy drink, a pre-workout, or a supplement. It is a category of its own, so don't label it as any of those. The hard rule: no medical claims. It does not cure, treat, or fix anything, and it is never tied to anxiety, ADHD, focus disorders, brain injury, concussions, or disease. Keep everything to how it feels: clean, calm focus.

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Say "brain fuel" at least once, every video.
  • Open on the problem or the feeling in the first 1 to 3 seconds.
  • Say "tap the orange cart" out loud before you finish.
  • Show yourself actually drinking it.
  • Keep it real and native, like a post, not an ad.

Don't

  • Don't open on energy drinks. They can sit inside your story, just never in the first line. Videos built around them are our worst performer by some distance.
  • Don't use vague urgency like "still in stock, barely." It returns about a fifth of what a real, named sellout does.
  • Open with caffeine or ketones. They come after the hook.
  • Call it an energy drink, pre-workout, or supplement.
  • Make medical claims or tie it to any condition, injury, or disease.

Video 1 — Best Flavor Yet 1.65x

The fun one to film. Raspberry Lychee, filmed before you open anything.
The hook

"I did not think a ketone drink could taste like this. Watch my face."

Or riff on these
01
“Raspberry Lychee has to be the new fave.”
Short, casual, works as on-screen text over a first sip.
02
“They just launched this one and it might be their best yet.”
Good if you would rather not put your face this close to camera.
Why this one works

Taste is the one thing people are unsure about before they buy, so clearing it on camera removes the last reason to hesitate. It works: two of the five best videos on this page are taste videos, and genuine surprise is something you cannot fake or script, which is exactly why viewers trust it. Your version genuinely shapes what we learn next.

The shot plan
1
Set the expectation low

First 1 to 3 seconds. Say what you assumed a ketone drink would taste like.

2
Crack it on camera

Close-up. Do not cut away. The sound of the can opening is part of it.

3
Film your actual first sip

Genuinely your first, if you can. Do not rehearse it and do not film take four.

4
Close

Name the flavor, say caffeine-free brain fuel, and "tap the orange cart" out loud.

Watch out on this one

Film it before you have tried the flavor, or the surprise is gone. If you have already had it, use one you have not opened yet. A performed reaction is obvious and it is the fastest way to lose the comments.

Video 2 — The Sellout 2.19x

Golden Pear sold out in four weeks. Now it is back. This one is the Golden Pear can.
The hook

"Golden Pear sold out in four weeks. It's finally back."

Or riff on these
01
“If you missed this one the first time, don't do that again.”
Cheekier. Only if it suits your voice.
02
“This one disappeared in four weeks. They finally made more.”
Same fact, softer delivery. Works as on-screen text over the can.
Why this one works

The best return of any opening we tested in August, and the one with the most behind it, which makes it the number we trust most on this page. Scarcity works here because it is true: Golden Pear really did sell out in four weeks, and the Raspberry Lychee launch really was pulled forward because of it.

The shot plan
1
Open on the sellout

First 1 to 3 seconds. Name the flavor that ran out and how fast.

2
Show what you have

Hold the can. Say which one you got and why you wanted it.

3
Bring in what it actually is

Caffeine-free brain fuel, 12g of ketones. One line, not a lecture.

4
Close

Say "tap the orange cart" out loud.

Watch out on this one

It has to be specific and it has to be true. This is the sharpest thing in our data: a real sellout, named, with a real timeframe, is our best performer. Vague urgency like "still in stock, barely" returns roughly a fifth of that. Same idea, eight times worse, because one is a fact and the other is a sales line people have heard a thousand times. This video is the Golden Pear one, and only Golden Pear. Raspberry Lychee has only just launched, so it has never sold out and you should never say it did. Never invent a sellout.

Video 3 — The Honest Take 1.25x

You level with people instead of reviewing at them. Either can, once you have tried both. This is the personal one.
The hook

"I'm just gonna be honest, these are genuinely some of the best caffeine-free focus drinks out right now."

Or riff on these
01
“I'll be straight with you about this one.”
Shorter. Lets your actual opinion carry the video.
02
“I don't post about everything I get sent. I'm posting about this one.”
Only use it if it's true for you.
Why this one works

"Let me be honest with you" is a different social move from a review. You are talking to one person, not an audience, and the viewer can hear the difference. By now you have tried both flavors, so you are not guessing, and that comes through. This is also the one where your own story does the selling: why you went looking in the first place, and what your afternoons actually feel like. This one is the newest of the three and it is climbing. Getting in early on it is the point.

What to actually talk about

After the honest opener, this is where you get personal. Any of these, in your own words.

01
Why you went looking in the first place
Cutting back on caffeine, trying to get off energy drinks, or just tired of how the afternoon goes.
02
What your 2pm actually feels like
The foggy hour, the second coffee that does nothing, rereading the same thing three times. Describe your day, not a symptom.
03
What you tried before this
And why it did not stick. Honest beats flattering here.
04
Where it fits in your day now
When you reach for it and what you swapped out. Specific times land better than "all day energy".
The shot plan
1
Open on the level-with-you line

To camera, first 1 to 3 seconds. Lower your voice a little. This is a conversation.

2
Say why you were looking

Cutting down on caffeine, the 2pm dip, wanting out of the energy drink habit. Your real reason, not a benefit list.

3
Give your actual opinion

What you think of it, including anything you would change.

4
Say what it is

Caffeine-free brain fuel, 12g of ketones.

5
Close

Say "tap the orange cart" out loud.

Watch out on this one

Honesty is the whole mechanism, so a performed version of it is worse than not doing it at all. If there is something you would change about the product, saying so makes the rest more believable, not less.

Still lead with the honest line, not with caffeine or energy drinks. Your reason for looking belongs in the story, never in the opening seconds. Videos that open on energy drinks are our weakest performer.

Describe your afternoon, not a condition. "I hit a wall at 2pm" is your experience and it is fine. Naming ADHD, brain fog as a diagnosis, or anything medical is not, and never say it treats, fixes or cures anything.

Before you post

Two minutes of checks that are worth more than another hour of filming.

Say "tap the orange cart" out loud. It is the strongest single signal in our entire shop data: videos that say it convert at 43% against about 10% for everything else, and earn roughly $210 a video against $25 to $35. Only 37 of our 3,326 videos have ever said it. It costs you one second.

Run through this on each video

Out loud, not just on screen.

6
Any medical language?

Nothing about treating, curing, fixing, or any condition. Keep it to how it felt.
