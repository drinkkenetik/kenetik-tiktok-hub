Make your first three videos

Three hooks, three videos, about an hour of filming. This page walks you through all of it.
Why only three

We have tested thousands of Kenetik videos on TikTok Shop, and three hooks pull away from everything else. Rather than hand you a library and hope, we are asking for one video on each. Same product, three different openings. Whichever one your audience responds to is the one we put real budget behind, and top videos graduate into our paid ads, where you earn commission again.

What Kenetik is (and isn't)

What Kenetik is

Metabolic brain fuel: clean, caffeine-free focus in a can or a shot. Steady, calm clarity that lasts, without the crash. 12g of ketones, caffeine-free, sugar-free, nothing artificial. Always lead with the person's problem or the moment they feel it, then bring in Kenetik as the fix.

What Kenetik is NOT

Not an energy drink, a pre-workout, or a supplement. It is a category of its own, so don't label it as any of those. The hard rule: no medical claims. It does not cure, treat, or fix anything, and it is never tied to anxiety, ADHD, focus disorders, brain injury, concussions, or disease. Keep everything to how it feels: clean, calm focus.

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Say "brain fuel" at least once, every video.
  • Open on the problem or the feeling in the first 1 to 3 seconds.
  • Say "tap the orange cart" out loud before you finish.
  • Show yourself actually drinking it.
  • Keep it real and native, like a post, not an ad.

Don't

  • Open with caffeine or ketones. They come after the hook.
  • Call it an energy drink, pre-workout, or supplement.
  • Make medical claims or tie it to any condition, injury, or disease.
  • Lead with the discount or over-produce it like a commercial.
  • Open on "I cut out energy drinks". It is our worst performer.
Your three videos

Tap any one to get the hook, the reason it works, and the shot plan.

One thing to know before you start

These are not scripts. Say them in your voice, to your people. What matters is that each video opens differently from the other two, because the whole point is finding out which opening your audience responds to. Same can, same story, three different first lines.

Video 1 — The Doubt

You thought it was nonsense. Then you tried it.
The hook

"I have tried a lot of things that did nothing. I did not expect this one to be different."

Or riff on these
01
“I wasn't expecting to like these as much as I do.”
The shortest version. Works as on-screen text over a plain shot.
02
“I genuinely thought this was going to be another thing I tried once and forgot about.”
Longer, more conversational. Good if you talk to camera a lot.
Why this one works

Leading with your own doubt disarms theirs. The viewer's first thought about any product like this is "that probably does nothing", and if you say it before they do, you are on their side of the screen instead of the brand's. In our paid data this framing returns roughly ten times what our weakest hook does, and it is the same structure behind a $127k video for another brand we audited. It is the strongest signal we have.

The shot plan
1
Open on the doubt

To camera, first 1 to 3 seconds. Say the line like you mean it, because you probably did think that.

2
Say what you expected

One sentence. What you assumed it would be, or what has let you down before.

3
What actually happened

Your honest experience. Where you were, what you were doing, what you noticed. Keep it to how it felt.

4
Close

Caffeine-free brain fuel, 12g of ketones, and "tap the orange cart" out loud.

Watch out on this one

The doubt has to be real. A performed eye-roll reads instantly as an ad and kills the whole thing. If you were never skeptical, say what you actually thought instead. And keep it to your own experience — never say the product treats, fixes, or cures anything.

Video 2 — The First Sip

Your real face, the first time you taste it.
The hook

"I did not think a ketone drink could taste like this. Watch my face."

Or riff on these
01
“Zero caffeine, zero sugar, 12 grams of ketones — and it tastes like this?”
Numbers first, taste as the payoff. The question mark is what makes it a hook.
02
“Everything healthy tastes like punishment. I was ready for that. Watch.”
Sets the expectation low so the reaction does the work.
Why this one works

Taste is the one objection every ketone drink has to clear, so clearing it on camera removes the only real reason someone hesitates. It is our highest-converting theme at 16%, earning around $82 a video against $18 for our weakest. Genuine surprise cannot be scripted, which is exactly why it converts — the viewer can tell the difference and rewards it.

The shot plan
1
Set the expectation low

First 1 to 3 seconds. Say what you assumed a ketone drink would taste like.

2
Crack it on camera

Close-up. Do not cut away. The sound of the can opening is part of it.

3
Film your actual first sip

Genuinely your first, if you can. Do not rehearse it and do not film take four.

4
Close

Name the flavor, say caffeine-free brain fuel, and "tap the orange cart" out loud.

Watch out on this one

Film it before you have tried the flavor, or the surprise is gone. If you have already had it, use a flavor you have not opened yet. A performed reaction is obvious and it is the fastest way to lose the comments.

Video 3 — Two Cans

Your old drink in one hand. Kenetik in the other.
The hook

"Two cans. One is 200 milligrams of caffeine and 39 grams of sugar. This one is zero, zero, and 12 grams of ketones."

Or riff on these
01
“Read the back of both of these with me.”
Slower, more curious. Let the labels carry it.
02
“Same size can. Completely different thing inside.”
Good if you would rather not read numbers out loud.
Why this one works

Two objects in frame do the arguing for you, and the viewer reaches the conclusion themselves instead of being told. That is why it beats any claim-led read. It is our best-performing format and we have barely touched it — run roughly 19 times in 5,000 videos — while two of the biggest brands on TikTok Shop use it as their hero format.

The shot plan
1
Both cans in frame

First 1 to 3 seconds, one in each hand. Do not explain yet, just hold them up.

2
Read the numbers slowly

One shocking figure beats four. Take your time on the sugar number.

3
Say which one you reach for now

And why, in your own words. This is where you show up, not the labels.

4
Close

Caffeine-free brain fuel, and "tap the orange cart" out loud.

Watch out on this one

Compare the numbers, never the brand. Do not name the other drink, do not say it is bad for you, and do not suggest it is unsafe. You are showing two labels and letting people read them. That is the whole move, and it is the part that keeps it fair.

Before you post

Two minutes of checks that are worth more than another hour of filming.
The one line most people forget

Say "tap the orange cart" out loud. It is the strongest single signal in our entire shop data: videos that say it convert at 43% against about 10% for everything else, and earn roughly $210 a video against $25 to $35. Only 37 of our 3,326 videos have ever said it. It costs you one second.

Run through this on each video
1
Does it open on a feeling, not an ingredient?

If the first line contains "ketones" or "caffeine", move it later.

2
Did you say "brain fuel" at least once?

This is the category we are building. It only works if everyone says it.

3
Did you say "tap the orange cart"?

Out loud, not just on screen.

4
Are all three openings different?

If two of your videos start the same way, you have only run one test.

5
Any medical language?

Nothing about treating, curing, fixing, or any condition. Keep it to how it felt.
