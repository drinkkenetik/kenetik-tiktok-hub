What Kenetik is (and isn't)

What Kenetik is

Metabolic brain fuel: clean, caffeine-free focus in a can or a shot. Steady, calm clarity that lasts, without the crash. 12g of ketones, caffeine-free, sugar-free, nothing artificial. Always lead with the person's problem or the moment they feel it, then bring in Kenetik as the fix.

What Kenetik is NOT

Not an energy drink, a pre-workout, or a supplement. It is a category of its own, so don't label it as any of those. The hard rule: no medical claims. It does not cure, treat, or fix anything, and it is never offered as the answer to anxiety, ADHD, focus disorders, brain injury, concussions, or disease. Describing what caffeine did to you is a different thing and it is fine: jittery, wired, anxious, could not sleep. Say that as your reason for switching. Just never put Kenetik on the other side of it as the fix.

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Say "brain fuel" at least once, every video.
  • Open on the problem or the feeling in the first 1 to 3 seconds.
  • Say "shop now" out loud before you finish.
  • Show yourself actually drinking it.
  • Say it in your own words. If it sounds like a script, rewrite it before you film.
  • Say what caffeine did to you (jittery, wired, could not sleep) as your reason for switching. Just never claim Kenetik treats it.
  • Keep it real and native, like a post, not an ad.

Don't

  • Don't lead with what you quit. Naming what you moved on from works inside your story and one borrowed line is fine, but a video whose whole first sentence is about another drink is our worst performer by some distance. Land on metabolic brain fuel out loud in the same video, every time.
  • Don't use vague urgency like "still in stock, barely." It returns about a fifth of what a real, named sellout does.
  • Open with caffeine or ketones. They come after the hook.
  • Call it an energy drink, pre-workout, or supplement.
  • Make medical claims or tie it to any condition, injury, or disease.

Video 1 — The Switch 3.9x

The strongest hook we have, on either platform. Any can you already drink. Say what you switched to, not just what you left.
The hook

"I used to run on three coffees before noon. I stopped, and this is the only reason it stuck."

Or riff on these
01
“I finally got my hands on the ones everybody keeps calling the energy drink killer.”
Borrowed verdict. Strongest single line in our data, and it shows up in three separate winners.
02
“Oh, we’re still drinking this. And my reason is simple.”
Understated. Works if you have posted about Kenetik before.
03
“A year ago I wanted to get off coffee. This is the only thing that made it stick.”
Your real timeline. Only use it if it is true.
Why this one works

This is the highest returning hook family we have, and it wins on TikTok Shop and in our paid ads at the same time. Nothing else does both. The versions that win all report a decision you already made. The versions that ask the viewer a question about what they drink are our weakest performers, so state it, do not ask it.

The shot plan
1
Open on the switch, already made

First 1 to 3 seconds, to camera. Past tense. You are reporting, not persuading.

2
Say what your day used to look like

One concrete detail beats a list. The 2:30 refill, the third cup, the drive home.

3
Say what you switched to, by name

This is the half people skip. Brain fuel is the destination. Hold the can, say it out loud, and make it impossible to miss what replaced what.

4
Make it concrete

Metabolic brain fuel, 12g of ketones, caffeine-free, and roughly when you made the change. Months, not “recently”.

5
Close

Say “shop now” out loud.

Watch out on this one

Half a switch is not a switch. If your video only says what you stopped, the viewer has nothing to move to. Name brain fuel as the thing you switched to, every time.

If you use the energy drink killer line, the video has to make clear what Kenetik actually is. The phrase describes what it replaced for you, never what it is. Land on metabolic brain fuel out loud, in the same video, every time.

Keep it to your own routine. What you cut back on is your story. Do not tell anyone else what to quit, and never frame it as an addiction, a craving, a dependency or withdrawal.

Say what caffeine did to you, not what Kenetik does for you. Jittery, wired, anxious, could not sleep: all fine as your reason for switching, because that is your own experience of a stimulant. What we cannot do is put Kenetik on the other side of it as the treatment. No health outcomes, no conditions, no before and after.

Video 2 — The Afternoon 1.8x

The best opening we have and the weakest ending. Any can, filmed at the actual time. The fog is a fuel problem, so say so.
The hook

"It's 3pm and I'm not tired. I'm foggy. There's a difference."

Or riff on these
01
“Everything on my list after 2pm takes twice as long.”
Names the cost, not the feeling. Good if you would rather not talk about yourself.
02
“This is the hour of the day I used to write off.”
Quiet open. Let the room and the light do the work.
03
“The 3pm fog is not a caffeine problem. Your brain is running on empty.”
Names the cause in the hook itself. The clearest version of this one.
Why this one works

42% of the people who start this one keep watching, the highest hold of any hook we run. It is the only hook here that beats the others on attention rather than on return. The catch is the ending: it holds people and then loses them, which is why the close matters more on this one than on either of the others. Give the last three seconds real effort.

The shot plan
1
Film it at the actual time

Mid afternoon, in the room where it happens. Do not shoot this fresh in the morning and pretend.

2
Open on the distinction

Not tired, foggy. That difference is the whole hook. Say it in the first 3 seconds.

3
Show the hour, do not describe it

The reread paragraph, the tab you keep reopening, the walk to the kitchen you did not need.

4
Say why it is happening

Your brain is underfuelled, not under-caffeinated. That is the whole point of the video. Tired is a sleep problem. Foggy is a fuel problem, and more of a stimulant does not fuel anything.

5
Bring it in as the fix

Metabolic brain fuel, caffeine-free. Crack it on camera if you have not already.

6
Land the close

Say “shop now” out loud, and mean it. This is the one that leaks.

Watch out on this one

Describe your afternoon, not a condition. “I hit a wall at 3” is your experience and it is fine. Naming brain fog as a diagnosis, or ADHD, or anything medical, is not.

The jitters and the wired feeling are fair game. If caffeine used to leave you restless, anxious or unable to sleep, say so in the body of the video. That is your experience of a stimulant and it is one of the most common real reasons people switch. Keep it as something caffeine did to you, never as something Kenetik treats.

Do not let it drift into a slump video. The hook is the distinction between tired and foggy. If you spend the whole video on how bad the afternoon is, there is nothing left to turn.

Do not frame it as needing more of a stimulant. The fog is not a caffeine shortage and the fix is not a stronger version of what was not working. It is fuel your brain can actually use. Get that distinction on camera or the video argues for the wrong product.

Video 3 — Best Flavor Yet 1.6x

The fun one to film. Raspberry Lychee, filmed before you open anything.
The hook

"I did not think a ketone drink could taste like this. Watch my face."

Or riff on these
01
“Did you know Kenetik came out with a Raspberry Lychee?”
Straight question about the flavor itself. Our best performing version of this one.
02
“Raspberry Lychee has to be the new favourite.”
Short, casual, works as on-screen text over a first sip.
Why this one works

Taste is the one thing people are unsure about before they buy, so clearing it on camera removes the last reason to hesitate. Genuine surprise is something you cannot fake or script, which is exactly why viewers trust it. This is the only shot on the page you cannot redo, so if you have not opened the Raspberry Lychee yet, film this one first.

The shot plan
1
Set the expectation low

First 1 to 3 seconds. Say what you assumed a ketone drink would taste like.

2
Crack it on camera

Close-up. Do not cut away. The sound of the can opening is part of it.

3
Film your actual first sip

Genuinely your first, if you can. Do not rehearse it and do not film take four.

4
Close

Name the flavor, say metabolic brain fuel, and “shop now” out loud.

Watch out on this one

Film it before you have tried the flavor, or the surprise is gone. If you have already had it, use one you have not opened yet. A performed reaction is obvious and it is the fastest way to lose the comments.

Raspberry Lychee has never sold out. Golden Pear did. Never move a sellout onto a flavor it did not happen to.

Before you post

Two minutes of checks that are worth more than another hour of filming.
The one line most people forget

Say “shop now” out loud. Saying your call to action rather than only putting it on screen is one of the strongest signals in our shop data, and “shop now” is the one phrase that works everywhere we run your video, on TikTok and in our paid ads. It costs you one second.

Run through this on each video
1
Does it open on a feeling or a fact, not an ingredient?

If the first line contains "ketones" or "caffeine", move it later.

2
Is anything you claimed actually true?

Especially the sellout. Never invent one.

3
Did you say "brain fuel" at least once?

This is the category we are building. It only works if everyone says it.

4
Did you say “shop now”?

Out loud, not just on screen.

5
Does it sound like you?

Read your first line back. If it sounds like a script you were handed, rewrite it in your own words before you film.

6
Are all three openings different?

If two of your videos start the same way, you have only run one test.

7
Any medical language?

Nothing about treating, curing, fixing, or any condition. Keep it to how it felt.
