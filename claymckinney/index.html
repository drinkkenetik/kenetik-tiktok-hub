Your 5 hooks
01
“The advice nobody gives you: your focus runs out before your motivation does. This is what I fuel it with.”
Video idea: Talk to camera, deliver the line like advice, then show the can.
Framing it as advice being withheld is Medicube's whole engine — the viewer stays because he thinks he is about to learn something, not buy something. The line underneath is a real reframe, since he has been blaming his motivation for years. Deliver it as advice and let it sit before the can appears.
02
“Before a long session, this is what I reach for. Focus that lasts, no junk.”
Video idea: Day-in-the-life lead-in, Kenetik before the work starts.
People who follow your journey buy what you actually use, not what you argue for, so showing the habit beats making the case. “No junk” is doing a lot of vague work, though. Land it on one of the approved specifics instead — caffeine-free, sugar-free, nothing artificial.
03
“I used to crash halfway through a big day. Changed one thing, now I finish as sharp as I started.”
Video idea: Voiceover over your day, the crash then the steady finish.
“Changed one thing” is an unfinished sentence, and unfinished sentences are hard to scroll past because the viewer stays to close them. Keep the payoff fast and keep it a story about how the day went, not a reveal of results. What you swapped is the interesting part; proving anything is not ours to do.
04
“Motivation gets you started. Clean fuel keeps you going. Here is what I use.”
Video idea: Talking head, motivational tone, honest product moment.
The parallel structure sounds like the language your people already follow, so the product walks in through a door they opened themselves. The risk is that it stays a poster. Follow it immediately with something specific and slightly unflattering from your own day, or the line floats off.
05
“No caffeine crash, no junk, just steady focus for the long haul. This is my daily.”
Video idea: Simple to-camera, the can in hand, your real routine.
“My daily” is the part that carries this. A routine is harder to argue with than a benefit, and repeat use is the implied proof. Crash-led openers are our lowest-converting theme at 7%, so lead with the routine and let the rest of the line arrive after.
Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Say "brain fuel" once.
  • Open on the crash or the long-session moment.
  • Keep it honest and first-person, your people trust your take.
  • Use day-in-the-life, voiceover, and talking head, all your strengths.
  • Show yourself actually drinking it.

Don't

  • Call it an energy drink (you're contrasting against them).
  • Make any health or medical claim.
  • Open with caffeine or ketones.
  • Over-produce it.
  • Cram everything into the opener.

The bottom line

Your lane is the trusted voice who found the clean fuel that keeps focus going for the long haul, no junk. Lead with the honest recommendation, name the crash, say brain fuel once. The videos that land, we run as paid ads, and that is how you keep earning.
