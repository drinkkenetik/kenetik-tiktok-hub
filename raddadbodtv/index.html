NYC Marathon Talking Points

No script. You're our Kenetik athlete heading into New York, so tell it the way you tell every story, in your own words. The examples below only show the idea.
How we think about Kenetik
Caffeine-free brain fuel for afternoon clarity and focus, without messing with your sleep.
The goal
When your audience hits the 2pm wall between work, the kids and the miles, and reaches for another coffee, we want them to think of Kenetik instead. New York is the backdrop. The afternoon is the story.
The 3 angles
1
Training around real life
WhyMost runner dads train in the gaps: before work, at lunch, after bedtime. The afternoon is where the day gets away from them, and the coffee that gets them through it is still working at midnight before a 5am long run. Show where Kenetik fits in your race week, and say what you used to grab instead.
What it could sound like (not a script)“The kids are down at 8, and that's when my marathon training actually starts. I used to hit a wall at 2pm and be checked out by dinner. Now it's one of these in the afternoon. Clear head, no caffeine, and nothing keeping me up before an early run.”
2
Off the caffeine treadmill
WhyCoffee to wake up, another at 2 to get through, wide awake at midnight, more coffee at 6. Your audience lives that loop, and as a runner you can name it better than anyone. Tell it as your own old habit, never a lecture.
What it could sound like (not a script)“I'll run on a treadmill all day. The caffeine treadmill is the one I had to get off. Coffee at 6, another at 2, wide awake at midnight, then do it all again. Now my afternoon is one of these, and the loop is gone.”
3
Packing for New York
WhyRace week is native content, so the shots can show up without a pitch: in the bag, at the hotel desk, before the shakeout run. This one works as a Reel opener and again as a travel story. Keep the race as the backdrop, never what Kenetik does for your running.
What it could sound like (not a script)“Packing for New York, and the shots go in the bag before the race shoes. Two ounces each, so they go right through security, and they're my afternoon move all week.”
How to close

Tag @BeKenetik on Instagram and send them to drinkkenetik.com with your link.

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Say caffeine-free first, then brain fuel and ketones if it comes up.
  • Film two or three different openers back to back, so we can test them as ads on your handle.
  • Keep it to your head and your afternoon: locked in, clear, there for the kids. Never faster, stronger or better recovered.
  • Only tell what's true for you, and skip any line that isn't your story.
  • Travel stories and the race vlog are up to you, wherever they fit naturally.

Don't

  • Call it an energy drink. It's caffeine-free brain fuel.
  • Make any health or medical claim, or say it helps you sleep.
  • Read it off a page. Tell it from how you actually use it.

The bottom line

Next time the 2pm wall hits between work, the kids and the miles, grab a Kenetik instead of another coffee. If your audience remembers one thing, that is it: a clear head all afternoon, with nothing in it keeping you up before an early run. How you show it is up to you.
