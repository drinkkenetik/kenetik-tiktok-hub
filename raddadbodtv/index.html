Your brief, Eric

Kenetik athlete, heading into the New York City Marathon. The runner dad who trains around real life and keeps his head clear for all of it.
The moment

The afternoon. Work, pickup, dinner, bedtime, and the run still to come. That is the stretch where most dads reach for another coffee and pay for it at midnight. You reach for Kenetik instead: locked in for the rest of the day, with nothing in it keeping you up before an early long run.

Who you're talking to

Runner dads and busy parents who train around work and family. Most of them run on coffee all day, hit the 2pm wall, and then lie awake when they should be asleep before a 5am run.

Your angle

Tell it the way you tell every story: the training, the kids, the work, and where Kenetik fits in your afternoon. New York is the backdrop. The afternoon lock-in is the point.

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Say caffeine-free first, and brain fuel once.
  • Film two or three openers back to back so we can test them as ads.
  • Keep it to your head and your afternoon: locked in, clear, present for the kids. Never faster, stronger or better recovered.
  • Only tell what's true for you, and skip any line that isn't your story.
  • Tag @drinkkenetik on the collab and on any travel stories.

Don't

  • Call it an energy drink (you're contrasting against them).
  • Make any health or medical claim.
  • Open with caffeine or ketones.
  • Over-produce it.
  • Cram everything into the opener.
