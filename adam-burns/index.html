Your brief, Adam

Built around the aid table, and around what our paid ads actually need.
The moment

Saturday morning, a folding table at the side of the trail, and a runner you have never met coming toward you with nothing left in the tank. You hand them something cold. For about four seconds they stop being alone with it. That is a moment almost nobody in our program can film, because almost nobody is standing where you are standing. Everything below is built on it.

Who you're talking to

Two audiences at once, and they need different things from you. The runner at your table has never heard of Kenetik, is out of breath, and will give you about five seconds before they move on. The person watching later is a runner, a hybrid athlete or someone who does hard things, and what convinces them is not you. It is the face of the stranger who just tried it.

Your angle

You are not reviewing a drink. You are giving one away and filming what happens. Lead with the table, let the runner react, and keep yourself mostly out of it. Metabolic brain fuel is the category, ketones are the mechanism, caffeine-free is the twist, and none of those lead. The generosity leads.

What to say at the table

This is the part that matters most. Say it out loud so the camera catches it, and keep it to these three beats.

1
As you hold it out, before they take it

“This one is not for your legs. It is for your head.” Seven words, sayable while they are still moving. It tells them it is not another electrolyte drink without you having to explain what it is not, and the “wait, what?” you get back is the shot.

2
When they ask what it is, and they will

“Brain fuel. Twelve grams of ketones, no caffeine. Tastes a lot better than that sounds.” One category, one checkable fact, and a last line that lowers their expectation right before they drink it. That gap between what they expect and what they get is the whole video.

3
As they drink it, say almost nothing

“Tell me what you think.” Then stop talking. A stranger saying “oh, that is actually good” mid-run is worth more than anything you can say to camera. Do not lead them and do not sell over the top of it.

Your hooks to camera

For the videos where you are talking, not handing out. Each one is a shape that is converting for us right now, rewritten around your table.

01
“I hand out free water to strangers every Saturday. There is one thing on my table I do not give away.”
Pattern interrupt  ·  Setting up the table  ·  Our strongest opening shape
The withhold does the work, and your own generosity is what stops it reading as a pitch. Film yourself setting up with the cans visible but unexplained, then pay it off. This shape is one of our best performing openings, and yours has a better reason to exist than most.
02
“About forty runners come through my table on a Saturday. You can tell instantly who still has a clear head and who is just putting one foot in front of the other.”
The thing only you can see  ·  At the table, runners passing  ·  Outcome-led
No other creator can say this line, because no other creator watches dozens of people hit the same point in the same state every week. It makes you a credible witness to the fog without you claiming anything about it. Say it plainly, no drama.
03
“I used to finish a long run and be useless for the rest of the day. I changed one thing about what I drink, and it was not cutting the miles.”
The switch, already made  ·  To camera, post-run  ·  Proven family
Past tense is what makes a switch believable, and the small turn at the end stops it sounding like a testimonial. Name what you switched to, out loud, or the video is only half a story. Keep it to your own Saturdays.
04
“Nobody expects the free table to have something they have never heard of.”
Curiosity, short  ·  Cold open on the table  ·  Works as on-screen text
A ten word open that earns the next five seconds and needs no setup. Good as the opening frame of a reaction compilation, where the payoff is three different runners trying it back to back.
05
“Handing a stranger something at mile eighteen and watching their face change is the best part of my weekend.”
The reason you do it  ·  To camera, warm  ·  Sets up the format
This is the one that makes the table a series rather than a one-off. It tells a new viewer what they are watching and why you are there, so use it on the video you would pin. It is also the most honest line on this page, which is why it will land.
Why this one matters for our ads

A stranger's unscripted reaction is the hardest thing in the world to fake, and it is the most valuable thing we can put in a paid ad. When a creator says a drink is good, viewers discount it. When someone who has never heard of the brand says it with a camera in their face at mile eighteen, they do not. Your top videos can graduate into our paid media, where you earn commission on them a second time.

1
Land the hook in the first three seconds

Paid gets scrolled harder than organic. If the first line arrives at second six, the ad is already dead.

2
Shoot vertical, and leave the top and bottom clear

We crop and add captions when it runs as an ad. Keep faces and the can out of the extreme edges.

3
Get a spoken yes on camera

A quick “mind if I film your reaction?” before they drink. We cannot run a stranger in a paid ad without it, and it almost never kills the moment.

4
Send us the raw file, not just the posted version

Posted clips come back compressed with the platform watermark, which we cannot use in paid.

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Say "brain fuel" out loud at least once, every video.
  • Film the runners who stop. The ones holding a pace give you a blur and no audio.
  • Let the reaction be the whole payoff. Silence is fine.
  • Say it in your own words. If a line here is not something you would actually say, change it.
  • Close on "shop now", spoken, not just on screen.

Don't

  • Suggest it helps anyone finish, recover, hydrate or perform. This is the one that will get an ad rejected.
  • Hand someone a can and then cut to them finishing strong. That edit implies a result we cannot claim.
  • Call it an energy drink, a supplement or a sports drink.
  • Open on ketones or caffeine. Both come after the hook, never in the first line.
  • Film anyone who has not said yes.

The short version

Set up the table like you always do. Say "this one is not for your legs, it is for your head" as you hand it over, answer with brain fuel, twelve grams of ketones, no caffeine, then ask what they think and stop talking. Get a yes before you film anyone, keep the hook inside three seconds, and send us the raw file. The reaction is the asset. Everything else on this page is just how to get out of its way.
