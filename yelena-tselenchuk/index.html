Your brief, Yelena

A naturopath's honest take, for women who read labels and ask questions.
The moment

The middle of a busy practice day, when you want to stay clear and present for every conversation without reaching for caffeine.

Who you're talking to

Women 30 to 50 in your Facebook groups who care about how they feel day to day, ask a lot of questions, and trust you because you read the label before they do.

Your angle

You're a practitioner, so lead with what you'd tell a client about your own routine: why you chose a caffeine-free drink, what's in it, and how you use it. Answer the questions your group already asks. Talk about your own experience and the ingredients, and never about treating or fixing anything.

What a great video covers
1Hook

Open in the first 1 to 3 seconds with one of your lines below.

2The problem

A long day of clients and questions, an afternoon dip in focus, and a group full of women tired of drinks that leave them wired.

3Kenetik

Crack one open, show your honest take. Say "brain fuel" once. Caffeine-free is the twist.

4Close

One reason to try it (caffeine-free, 12g of ketones) and your code GLOW or your link.

Talk to camera and simple label close-ups work best for you. Facebook group posts can be a short video plus a written answer to the most common question. Keep every word to your own experience and the label.

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Say brain fuel once.
  • Lead with your practitioner's eye and the label.
  • Answer the questions your groups actually ask.
  • Keep it to your own experience and what's in the can.

Don't

  • Call it an energy drink (you're contrasting against them).
  • Make any health or medical claim.
  • Open with caffeine or ketones.
  • Over-produce it.
  • Cram everything into the opener.

The bottom line

Your lane is the practitioner who reads the label first and shares what she actually drinks: caffeine-free brain fuel. Answer your group's questions, show the label, and keep it to your own experience.
